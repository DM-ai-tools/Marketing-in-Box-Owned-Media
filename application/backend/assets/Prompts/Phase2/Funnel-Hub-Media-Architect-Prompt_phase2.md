# FUNNEL HUB & MEDIA ARCHITECT — INPUT-DRIVEN PROMPT

A selection step and a three-phase master prompt for mapping the funnel types that fit a client's sub-service line, in three progressively deeper structures: media-fit grouping, a convergent "hub" model, and a customer-commitment ladder. The prompt is industry-agnostic; the funnels it keeps are not. Fill in the INPUTS block for any client, vertical, or sub-service, and the selection step decides which funnel types that business can actually run.

## STEP 0 — FUNNEL SELECTION (BEFORE PHASE 1)

**Governing rule for every step and phase below:** ground every topic, offer, and headline in the reference folder — competitor analysis, topic clusters, keyword research, personas, and any existing funnel or lead-magnet content already built for this client. Do not invent generic funnel content unrelated to what the client's actual strategy documents say. If an asset is referenced in those documents as "already in-market" but cannot be located as a standalone file, say so explicitly and rebuild the relevant piece fresh rather than assuming it exists.

The funnel types below are a **menu, not a required output**. The INPUTS and the industry voice guidance supplied with this prompt decide what this business may sell and how; the menu does not. Do not map a funnel type because it is on the menu.

**Funnel type menu:**
Lead Magnet / Squeeze Page, Reverse Squeeze Page, Survey Funnel, Tripwire / SLO Funnel, Free-Plus-Shipping / Book Funnel, Cart Funnel, VSL / Sales Letter Funnel, Product Launch Funnel, Challenge Funnel, Webinar Funnel, Summit Funnel, Application Funnel, Membership Funnel, High Ticket Funnel, Consultation Funnel, Free Trial Funnel, Evergreen Funnel, Quiz Funnel.

1. **Read before selecting:** the sub-service or product line being funnel-mapped, the ICP Document, the Existing Funnel Document, the offer ladder if it is in the reference material, and the industry voice guidance.
2. **Keep a funnel type only when all three are true:**
   - it fits how this business sells this sub-service — its sales motion, price point, delivery model, and any compliance limits the inputs or the industry voice guidance state;
   - the buyer described in the ICP can enter it — they would plausibly give what it asks for (an email, money, time, an application) at the point they meet it;
   - a real topic or offer for it already exists in the reference material, or can be built from facts those documents state.
3. **Drop every other type.** A dropped type is not grouped, mapped, ranked, drawn, or given offers anywhere in Phases 1–3.
4. **Publish the selection before any mapping**, as the first section of the output:
   - **Kept:** every funnel kept in the selection, by name, each with one line saying which of the three tests it passes and on what evidence.
   - **Not used:** each dropped type listed once, with one sentence naming the input that rules it out (for example the sub-service, the ICP, the Existing Funnel Document, the offer ladder, or the industry voice guidance).
   - If fewer than two types survive, say so and build only those. Do not refill the set to reach the size of the menu.

Different businesses keep different subsets. A regulated lender, a professional practice, and a product shop will not keep the same funnels; decide from this client's inputs, never from a default list.

## PHASE 1 — MEDIA FIT, TOPIC MAPPING & PURCHASE-INTENT RANKING

1. **Group every funnel kept in the selection by acquisition media** — Organic, Paid, or Both — based on the mechanics of each funnel type, not on where it could theoretically ever appear. State the reasoning for each: does the funnel's economics, format, or traffic-source need favour organic content/SEO, paid ad spend, or work equally for either? A media category that contains no kept funnel is omitted.
2. **Map every funnel kept in the selection to a real topic and offer for [sub-service / product line], grounded in the reference folder.** Each mapping should name: the specific topic or content asset it uses, what the funnel actually contains (steps, screens, or content sequence), and one to two offer headlines.
3. **Design the result as an interactive HTML mindmap** with expandable/collapsible nodes. Structure: a root node, branching into the media categories that hold at least one kept funnel (Organic / Both / Paid), each expanding to its member funnels; clicking a funnel node reveals its topic, content details, and offer(s). The mindmap contains only funnels kept in the selection. Reuse the client's existing design tokens (colours, type, button/card styles) rather than inventing a new visual system.
4. **Deliver a companion Word document** containing the selection (Kept and Not used), the media-fit grouping (with reasoning), the topic/content mapping for every funnel kept in the selection, and a ranking of every funnel kept in the selection by closeness to purchase intent — i.e. how far along a visitor already is toward being ready to transact when they enter that funnel type, independent of how large the funnel's ask is. Give a one-to-two-sentence reason for every rank.

---

## PHASE 2 — THE CONVERGENT HUB MODEL ("ALL ROADS LEAD TO ROME")

Restructure every funnel kept in the selection — and the offers/headlines built in Phase 1 — into a hub model. No dropped type appears in the hub:

1. **Entry and nurture stages carry multiple funnels.** The first two or three funnel stages (top-of-funnel and middle-of-funnel) should each contain several different kept funnel types, built for different audiences, formats, or channels, wherever several were kept.
2. **All of them converge into a single convert stage carrying only one or two main offers.** This is the "Rome" every road leads to.
3. **Post-conversion, multiple funnels/offers cascade as upsells and cross-sells** across one or more subsequent stages, forming a visible hub-and-cascade shape rather than a single straight-line funnel. Use only kept funnels and offers the reference material supports.
4. **Visualise the hub** — a diagram showing the many-to-few convergence into the convert stage, followed by the upsell/cross-sell cascade.
5. **Layer in three new metrics on every funnel/offer:**
   - **Ease of Implementation** — a simple score (e.g. 1–5) for how much build effort the funnel requires before it can run.
   - **Time Decay + Effort** — is the underlying topic/funnel/offer a temporary or viral asset (short shelf life, needs refreshing) or an evergreen one (durable, reusable indefinitely for the agency), cross-referenced against how much production effort it took to build (Low/Medium/High).
   - **Audience Awareness Level** — classify each funnel/offer along Unaware → Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware → Product-Aware → Most-Aware.
6. **Cross-map awareness level against relevance-by-role**, split roughly 50% business owners / 30% reviewers or coordinators / remainder technical or implementation stakeholders (adjust the named roles to whatever buyer personas exist for this client — if a technical/implementation-focused role doesn't already exist as a named persona, define it as a lightweight overlay rather than inventing a full new persona).
7. **Deliver a new Word document** presenting the hub model — visualised, staged, and populated with the topics/offers/headlines from Phase 1 — with all three new metrics attached to every funnel kept in the selection.

---

## PHASE 3 — THE LOW-TICKET-TO-HIGH-TICKET COMMITMENT LADDER

Re-group every funnel kept in the selection a second way — by **customer commitment**, not media or funnel stage:

1. **Order every funnel kept in the selection from lowest to highest commitment**, judged from the customer's point of view: what are they being asked to give up to enter this funnel — an email address, a few minutes, personal/business details, real money, real time, or some combination? (Reference example: a Lead Magnet Funnel asks only for an email; a Summit Funnel asks for contact details *and* a real time commitment — that is a higher-commitment ask even though no money changes hands.) The ladder contains only kept funnels.
2. **For every funnel in the ladder, supply two to three concrete offers**, each with its own headline and one-line description.
3. **Tag every offer with the same metrics introduced in Phase 2**: Time Decay + Effort, Audience Awareness Level, and the owner/reviewer/technical relevance split.
4. **Deliver this as an interactive HTML design only** — no Word document for this phase. Visualise the ascending commitment scale (e.g. a ladder, staircase, or ordered scale) with expandable detail per funnel, reusing the client's established design tokens.

---

## Notes for whoever executes this prompt

- Step 0 and the phases build on each other — Phases 1–3 run only on every funnel kept in the selection, and Phase 2 and 3 should reuse the topic/offer work from Phase 1 rather than inventing a second set of offers from scratch, unless the commitment re-grouping in Phase 3 genuinely calls for different offers at that ask level.
- Where the client's existing strategy documents reference assets as already built that cannot be located as real files, flag this explicitly rather than silently assuming they exist.
